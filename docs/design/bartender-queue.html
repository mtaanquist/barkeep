Barkeep · Back Bar · bartender queue

The queue, read at arm’s length with both hands full

The guest card is a signal; this is an instrument. Same tokens, opposite job: nothing here decorates, every row answers what do I do next and where do I tap. Two sizes — 1280 desktop and 820 tablet landscape, the one that is actually propped against a bottle rail. Danish shipped, English under each block.

Four rules the whole queue is built on

1 · Two of five statuses get a tint. Three don’t. A row fill is expensive: five tinted fills make a rainbow, and in dark mode five near-black tints are indistinguishable at arm’s length anyway. So status is carried by a 4px left rail (border-width-rail) plus the pill — new gets an ink rail, accepted a mid-grey rail, both on plain surface-raised. Only ready takes a fill (accent rail + a 6%-accent row wash) and only rejected takes the red one, and it is on screen for 8 seconds before it leaves. A queue of eight is therefore mostly white paper with two lit rows.
2 · One primary action per row, always in the same place, always the same shape. Rightmost, 220×56, filled text/text-inverse, verb only: Accepter → Meld klar → Ekspederet. Muscle memory finds the right edge of the row; the label tells you which verb you’re firing. Never accent: yellow means this drink is ready, and a yellow button on a row that isn’t ready breaks that promise.
3 · Reject is a different shape, a different size, and 20px away. 56×56 square, outline only, icon only, separated from the primary by a gap wider than a fingertip. Shape and position distinguish it even in the dark, and it is the only control on the row that opens a confirm dialog — so a mis-tap costs one more tap, not one drink.
4 · The list never reorders under a hand. Oldest at the top, arrivals appended at the bottom, always. Nothing ever jumps above the row being worked. The only thing that moves on arrival is a count in the header and a single hairline flash down the new row’s rail.

820 · tablet landscape · the realistic case

At 820 the status column is the first thing to go — the pill moves under the drink name, on the same line as the guest, and the rail plus the primary verb carry the state instead. Actions keep their exact geometry (56² reject, 20px gap, filled primary) because that is the muscle memory; the primary narrows to 180px, which still fits Ekspederet at 17px. Recipes at this width become two columns, method left, ingredients and glass stacked right, and the type does not shrink: 17px is the floor.

One order · empty · and an order arriving mid-pour

One order keeps the full row geometry — the primary button does not grow to fill the panel, because the hand learns one target size and one target position. The panel just gets short.
Empty is not a celebration and not an illustration: the count reads 0, one line says the bar is caught up, and the last completed orders stay on screen — the most useful thing in an empty queue is proof of what just went out.
Arrival is the one live event that must not steal focus. The new row is appended at the bottom, never inserted; the header count ticks 3 → 4; the new row’s rail flashes accent once and fades to ink over 900ms; and if the queue is scrolled past the bottom, a 44px pill appears at the foot of the list — 1 ny nedenfor — instead of scrolling anything. No sound, no toast over the queue, no reordering. The row being poured does not move by a pixel.
