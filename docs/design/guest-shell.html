Barkeep · Front of house · the guest shell

Your drink follows you.

One shell, three surfaces, and a dock that carries the live order across all of them. The guest side has exactly one thing it must never fail to say — din drink er klar — so the dock is the fixed point and everything else is arranged around it: history slides over the menu, the recipe slides over the menu, and both stop short of the dock. Nothing in the guest app is allowed to cover it.

Four rules

01 · THE DOCK IS THE APP

The in-progress order lives in a fixed strip at the bottom of the viewport, on every screen, at every width. It is not a card inside the menu that scrolls away. Reading a recipe, scrolling history, six screens deep — the dock is there, and it is what turns yellow.

02 · OVER, NEVER INSTEAD

History and recipes are overlays with the menu alive behind them. A guest who scanned a QR code 40 seconds ago has no mental model to lose their place in, so we never take the place away.

03 · CHIPS FILTER, CHROME NAVIGATES

Pill-shaped, in the content column, ink-filled when on → changes the list under it. Square 3px, in the header or dock, always with an icon and a labelled way back → changes the surface. A filter never appears in the chrome; navigation is never a pill.

04 · ONE FILL, ONE MEANING

Accent yellow means ready, go and get it — and only that. Ink fill means this is the action. Everything else is a hairline. The dock going yellow is therefore legible from a metre away, out of focus, in a dark room.

390 · the shell, and the dock in its three sizes

Header is one line of identity, one line of welcome, and one square icon button — history. The old logout link beside it is gone (see the last section). Under it, the connection banner slot; under that, the filter rail, which is the menu's own and belongs to the content, not the chrome. The dock sits at the foot: absent when there is no order, a 68px strip while it is being made, and expanded and yellow when it is ready — the only unprompted state change in the guest app. Tapping the strip expands it to the full status card; tapping again collapses. Ready ignores that and expands itself.

“LOGOUT” — WHAT IT ACTUALLY IS

A guest typed a first name. There is no account, no password, nothing to protect. So Log ud is answering a question nobody asked, in the language of a bank, next to the thing they actually wanted.

The real want is “not me” — a phone handed to a friend, or a name typed wrong. That is rare, mildly destructive, and belongs at the bottom of a screen you have to deliberately open. Hence: no chrome presence at all, a muted outline button under a rule at the foot of past orders, and a dialog that names the consequence in guest language.

“Leave this bar” is not a thing guests do. They walk away and the tab dies. Deliberately not designed — no exit, no goodbye screen. Bar-switching happens by scanning a different QR code, which already works.

Accident-proofing at 390: two taps deep, 48px, muted, no fill, and the destructive option in the dialog is the outline one while Bliv som Kasper takes the ink fill — the only place in the product where the safe choice is also the loud one.

390 · the recipe, over the menu

A sheet rising from the bottom to 88% height, the menu dimmed but legible above it, a drag handle and a 44px close. With a photo it leads with the photo, cropped to a fixed 180px band, which is how user-uploaded photos of every aspect ratio stay deliberate. Without one the drink's name is set at 32px on a sunken band of the same height — the layout doesn't change shape, so a bar with no photos looks designed rather than broken. Either way the sheet stops above the dock, and the Order button repeats at the sheet's foot so reading a recipe ends in the action it leads to.

1280 · same shell, the dock becomes a column

At desktop the menu already has a 248px left nav for filters, so the dock moves to a 288px right column — sticky, full status card, always visible, no collapsed state needed. History opens as a panel over the grid from the right, stopping short of the dock column; the recipe is a centred dialog with the grid dimmed behind it. The filter nav on the left and the dock on the right make rule 03 spatial: filters live left, your order lives right, and the middle is the menu.

RECONCILING THE GUEST MENU & ORDER STATUS
Guest menuRevise the header. The two icon buttons become one (history); the logout icon is deleted outright. The status card that used to sit inline at the top of the list is removed — it is the dock now, and having both is two sources of truth. Filter rail, cards, and the disabled-Order treatment are untouched.
Order statusKeep entirely, re-house it. All five states, the track, and the accepted→ready frame sequence survive as designed — they now render inside the dock rather than in the menu's flow, which means the “order placed” confirmation is the dock appearing (68px slide up, 220ms) rather than a card scrolling into view. Same idea, better home.
Connection bannerShell-owned, single instance. It lives directly under the header on every guest screen, so the drift where past orders had no banner cannot happen again. When the socket drops, the dock's status line changes to Mister forbindelsen — vi prøver igen and the track stops animating rather than lying.
Deleted: the second past-orders header, the header logout link, the blank-backdrop recipe modal, and the inline status card in the menu. Four things replaced by two: the shell and the dock.
