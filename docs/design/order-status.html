Barkeep · Back Bar · guest order status

The card that sits at the top of the menu until the drink is in your hand

One component, five states, built from tokens.css and the existing kit — status pill, card, dialog, button. It never navigates: it changes under the guest's thumb. Progress is carried by a four-step track, not by colour, so four of the five states can stay on the neutral ramp and ready can own the accent alone. Danish shipped, English under each block.

Anatomy & the progress track

Track — four segments, 6px tall, 3px apart, full card width: Modtaget · Laves · Klar · Afhentet. Segments the order has reached — including the one it is sitting in — are solid text; the ones ahead are border. Filled count is the step number: one of four is new, two is being made, three is ready. No third treatment for "current", because at a glance a half-filled bar reads as an ambiguous fourth colour. Position in the sequence is legible at a glance, upside down, at arm's length — no reading required.
Body — mono kicker (order id · time), text-heading drink name, then the status in words at text-body. The pill repeats the state as a token so it matches the bartender's rail exactly.
Action — one 56px full-width cancel, border outline, never a danger fill: cancelling your own drink is not a destructive act, it is a change of mind. It is present in new, accepted and ready, and gone in processed and rejected — the card's footer rule disappears with it, so the card physically shrinks when the order is closed.

Five states · dark

Dark is the real room, not a preference. Three things change beyond token swaps: ready’s halo becomes an outward glow rather than a hard ring, because on near-black a ring reads as a border and a glow reads as a lamp; processed drops to surface-sunken so a finished order sinks below the page instead of floating on it; and rejected keeps its salmon foreground but never gets a fill brighter than the accent — nothing in the product is allowed to out-shine READY.

Ready — five mechanisms, no confetti

A phone comes out of a pocket into a dark loud room and is glanced at for under a second, often at a bad angle. The old green card with a 🎉 fails that glance twice: green is a mood, not a signal, and an emoji is 20px of detail. Instead the whole screen changes state:
1 · The card takes the accent. It is the only accent fill in the entire product, so the yellow is the message. 9.6:1 in light, 11.6:1 in dark.
2 · The menu behind it dims. Everything below the card drops to 35% and its Order buttons go disabled — the card becomes the only lit object on the screen, which is what the eye finds first at low light.
3 · The instruction is the headline. 40px, two words of verb: Hent den i baren. The drink name demotes to the line under it, and a spoken pickup token — “sig fjorten” — replaces reading a number off a screen at a crowded bar.
4 · A slow halo, 1.8s. One breath per cycle, no colour change, no bounce: motion in peripheral vision is what catches a glance, and slow motion survives being seen at 15fps through a pocket. It stops after 30 seconds — a beacon that never stops is noise.
5 · The device does the rest. Vibration on state entry and a system notification with the same words, so the mechanism doesn’t depend on the app being open or on sound.

The halo blooms once on arrival, then settles into the 1.8s breath, and the menu below dims to 35% across the same 400ms. One haptic tap fires at 0ms, not at the end.

The other transitions use the same grammar, shorter. new → accepted and ready → processed are 160ms: track segment sweeps, words crossfade, no surface change, no halo. rejected is the one interruption — it enters at 240ms with a 4px downward settle instead of a rise, the only movement in the system that goes down. prefers-reduced-motion: sweeps and rises become instant crossfades at duration-quick; the halo becomes a static 3px accent ring. Nothing is lost, because the halo was never carrying the message alone.

“Order placed” — the confirmation is the card arriving

No toast, no full-screen success page, no interstitial to dismiss. Tapping Bestil scrolls the menu to the top and the status card drops in from under the header over duration-enter — the guest watches their tap become the thing that will now live at the top of their screen. For its first 4 seconds the card wears a confirmation strip that then retires, leaving the plain new state; the strip is the only place the word “sendt” appears, because after 4 seconds it is no longer news. Every Order button on the menu goes disabled in the same 160ms with one line of explanation, so the disabled state never looks like a bug.

“Overrask mig” — the reveal

The dialog component, unchanged: overlay backdrop, surface-raised panel, radius-lg, shadow-float. What makes it a reveal is the copy and the order of the three actions: the drink name arrives at text-display — bigger than it ever appears on a menu card — under a mono kicker that says who chose. Bestil is the 64px accent action and sits at the bottom where the thumb already is; Prøv en anden is an outlined 56px re-roll directly above it, because rejecting the suggestion is the second most likely thing a guest does, not a corner case; Annullér is a plain 44px text button, quiet but never hidden behind an X alone. Re-rolling swaps only the photo, name and ingredients — panel size and button positions are frozen, so the third re-roll is as fast to tap as the first.
